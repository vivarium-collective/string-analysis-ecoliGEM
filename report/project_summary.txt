WHAT WE ARE DOING
-----------------
This project uses genome-scale metabolic modeling (GEM) to build a simulated
EPS-producing E. coli cell. The goal is to predict EPS production rates under
different nutrient and regulatory conditions, giving quantitative estimates to
guide lab experiments.

A GEM model is a mathematical steady-state mass balance of all reactions in the
cell. Flux Balance Analysis (FBA) finds the reaction rates (fluxes) that satisfy
all balances while maximizing a chosen objective (biomass or EPS). Outputs are in
mmol/gDW/h, which convert to per-cell amounts using E. coli dry weight ~280 fg/cell.

Three complete EPS biosynthetic routes were added to iML1515 so the model can
measure EPS secretion as a real, quantifiable output across three EPS types:
  1. Cellulose (bcsA pathway)
  2. Colanic acid (Wca pathway)
  3. PNAG - poly-beta-1,6-GlcNAc (pgaC/pgaB pathway)

WHY THE ORIGINAL MODEL COULDN'T MAKE EPS
-----------------------------------------
Three problems prevented EPS production in the base iML1515 model:

1. Dead-end metabolite: UDPGPT (wcaJ) makes udcpgl_c, but nothing downstream
   consumes it. The Wca polymerization steps are absent from iML1515. FBA forces
   any reaction whose product has no drain to carry zero flux.

2. No export route: FBA can only measure a compound as "produced" if there is an
   exchange reaction (EX_xxx_e) representing secretion into the medium. Without
   an exchange reaction, no carbon can leave as EPS regardless of upstream flux.

3. c-di-GMP disconnected: ydaM (DGUNC) makes c-di-GMP and yahA/gmr degrade it,
   but nothing in the original model required c-di-GMP to make EPS. So the
   signaling pathway cycled without effect on EPS production.

Early work used a sink on udcpgl_c (SK_udcpgl_c) as a rough proxy -- just
draining the precursor to allow upstream reactions to run. This gave approximate
flux estimates but was not biologically meaningful as an EPS output.


THE THREE EPS PATHWAYS ADDED
------------------------------

=== PATHWAY 1: CELLULOSE (bcsA) ===

Biology:
  bcsA encodes cellulose synthase, which polymerizes UDP-glucose into beta-1,4-
  glucose chains (cellulose). bcsA binds c-di-GMP allosterically -- high c-di-GMP
  activates the enzyme. This is the major matrix polysaccharide in E. coli biofilms.

Stoichiometry source:
  The core reaction is the well-characterized bcsA enzymatic step:
  UDP-glucose --> [glucose unit in chain] + UDP
  Per monomer: one UDP-glucose consumed, one UDP released.
  The c-di-GMP requirement is modeled as a small stoichiometric cofactor
  (coefficient 0.001 per monomer) rather than a true allosteric activator,
  because FBA cannot represent allosteric regulation directly. This forces DGUNC
  (ydaM) to supply c-di-GMP for the reaction to carry flux.

Reactions added (all new to iML1515):
  CELSYNTH:     udpg_c + 0.001 cdigmp_c  -->  cellulose_c + udp_c
  CELLULOSEt:   cellulose_c  -->  cellulose_e
  EX_cellulose_e: cellulose_e  -->  [secreted]

New metabolites added:
  cellulose_c   formula C6H10O5  (cytoplasm, one anhydroglucose monomer unit)
  cellulose_e   formula C6H10O5  (extracellular)

How the EPS phenotype is simulated:
  Knocking out CDGUNPD + LDGUNPD (yahA and gmr reactions set to zero flux)
  blocks c-di-GMP degradation. All c-di-GMP made by DGUNC (ydaM) must then
  flow through CELSYNTH -- the only remaining drain. This mimics the high-c-di-GMP
  switch that triggers biofilm formation in E. coli.

=== PATHWAY 2: COLANIC ACID (Wca pathway) ===

Biology:
  Colanic acid is the primary EPS of E. coli K-12 under stress. It is a
  heteropolysaccharide with a repeating unit of approximately:
  [Glc - Gal - GlcA - Fuc - Fuc] with a pyruvate modification on the outer fucose.
  The Wca operon (wcaA through wcaL) assembles this on an undecaprenyl-phosphate
  lipid carrier in the inner membrane, then exports it.

Stoichiometry source:
  The colanic acid repeating unit composition is well-established in the literature
  (Stevenson et al. 1996, J Bacteriol): 1 Glc, 1 Gal, 1 GlcA, 2 Fuc, 1 pyruvate.
  In vivo, the Wca machinery assembles this on the undecaprenyl-PP lipid carrier.
  We model the full assembly as one lumped polymerization reaction (COLASYNTH)
  consuming the activated sugar nucleotides and PEP (pyruvate source):
    - udcpgl_c: the wcaJ/UDPGPT product -- glucose starter on undecaprenyl carrier
    - udpgal_c: galactose unit (from UDP-galactose, already in iML1515)
    - udpglcur_c: glucuronate unit (from UDP-glucuronate, already in iML1515)
    - gdpfuc_c: 2x fucose units (from GDP-fucose, already in iML1515)
    - pep_c: phosphoenolpyruvate (source of pyruvate modification, already in model)
  Products:
    - colacid_c: colanic acid repeating unit (approximate formula C33H52O27)
    - udcpp_c: undecaprenyl-phosphate recycled back to wcaJ
    - 2 udp_c: released when Gal and GlcA are added
    - 2 gdp_c: released when the two Fuc are added
    - pi_c: phosphate released from PEP during pyruvylation

  Why use udcpgl_c as the starter:
    wcaJ (UDPGPT) was a dead-end in the original model. Using its product as the
    glucose starter in COLASYNTH "unblocks" that dead-end -- the wcaJ reaction
    now serves a productive purpose in the model.

  Why lump all Wca steps into one reaction:
    The full Wca pathway has ~10 enzymatic steps (wcaB, wcaC, wcaD, wcaE, wcaF,
    wcaI, wcaJ, wcaK, wcaL, etc.). Most of these genes and their intermediate
    metabolites are not in iML1515. Lumping is standard practice in GEM expansion
    when the intermediate steps are not individually constrained.

Reactions added (all new to iML1515):
  COLASYNTH:    udcpgl_c + udpgal_c + udpglcur_c + 2 gdpfuc_c + pep_c
                    --> colacid_c + udcpp_c + 2 udp_c + 2 gdp_c + pi_c
  COLAIDt:      colacid_c  -->  colacid_e
  EX_colacid_e: colacid_e  -->  [secreted]

New metabolites added:
  colacid_c   formula C33H52O27  (cytoplasm, one repeating unit)
  colacid_e   formula C33H52O27  (extracellular)

Note on the formula C33H52O27:
  Approximate, calculated from the monomer composition:
  1 Glc (C6H10O5) + 1 Gal (C6H10O5) + 1 GlcA (C6H8O6) + 2 Fuc (C12H20O8)
  + pyruvate residue (C3H4O3) = C33H52O27. The exact formula does not affect
  FBA flux results; it matters only for elemental balance checks.

=== PATHWAY 3: PNAG (pgaC / pgaB) ===

Biology:
  PNAG (poly-beta-1,6-N-acetylglucosamine) is a surface-associated polysaccharide
  that promotes cell-cell adhesion in early biofilm. pgaC polymerizes UDP-GlcNAc
  into the PNAG chain. pgaB partially deacetylates it to increase adhesion.
  PNAG is regulated by c-di-GMP at the post-transcriptional level (IcaA-like
  mechanism). Importantly, its monomer (UDP-GlcNAc) contains nitrogen -- this
  means PNAG synthesis competes for nitrogen under N-limitation.

Stoichiometry source:
  The pgaC reaction is already partially represented in iML1515 as PUACGAMS:
    PUACGAMS: puacgam_c + udp_c <=> uacgam_c  (reversible)
  Running this in reverse gives synthesis:
    uacgam_c  -->  puacgam_c + udp_c
  UDP-GlcNAc (uacgam_c) is consumed and PNAG (puacgam_c) is produced.
  The periplasmic transport was also already present:
    PUACGAMtr: puacgam_c --> puacgam_p  (already in iML1515)

  What was missing: only the extracellular export step.

Reactions added (only 2 new reactions needed):
  PUACGAMex:    puacgam_p  -->  puacgam_e
  EX_puacgam_e: puacgam_e  -->  [secreted]

Reactions already in iML1515 used by this pathway:
  PUACGAMS (reverse): uacgam_c --> puacgam_c + udp_c  (pgaC -- synthesis)
  PUACGAMtr:          puacgam_c --> puacgam_p          (periplasm transport)

New metabolites added:
  puacgam_e   (extracellular PNAG)


MODELING APPROACH AND WORKFLOW
--------------------------------
All analyses use a two-stage optimization:
  Stage 1: Maximize biomass (parFBA) -- record max growth rate mu_max
  Stage 2: Constrain biomass >= 90% x mu_max, then maximize EPS exchange flux

The 90% constraint keeps the cell growing while allowing EPS production. This
reflects the biology: we want a growing cell that also makes EPS, not one that
has arrested growth to maximize EPS.

For each EPS pathway, the optimization objective is set to that pathway's
exchange reaction (EX_cellulose_e, EX_colacid_e, EX_puacgam_e). Results are
reported per-pathway rather than as a combined objective, because combining them
would require arbitrary weighting.


SIX CONDITIONS TESTED
----------------------
All conditions use M9 minimal medium as the base. Values are upper bound
constraints on exchange reaction uptake rates (mmol/gDW/h):

  1. baseline:       standard M9, glucose 10 mM, unlimited O2/N/P/S
  2. high_glucose:   glucose 20 mM (double carbon)
  3. low_oxygen:     O2 limited to 5 mM (semi-anaerobic)
  4. N_limited:      ammonium limited to 2 mM (nitrogen stress)
  5. EPS_phenotype:  CDGUNPD + LDGUNPD knocked out (high c-di-GMP state)
  6. combined_EPS:   high glucose + N-limited + c-di-GMP knockouts

The "combined_EPS" condition mimics the natural biofilm trigger in E. coli:
carbon excess (high glucose) + nitrogen stress (cannot use carbon for protein
growth) + c-di-GMP switch on (biofilm mode activated).

KEY BIOLOGICAL INSIGHTS FROM THE MODEL
---------------------------------------
1. Cellulose dominates: accounts for ~83% of total EPS in the combined_EPS
   condition. This matches published biofilm matrix composition data.

2. N-limitation is the strongest single driver: N_limited alone gives 8.79
   mmol/gDW/h total EPS vs 1.75 at baseline. When nitrogen is scarce, excess
   carbon cannot be used for protein/growth and is redirected to EPS.

3. c-di-GMP signal alone is insufficient: EPS_phenotype (only yahA/gmr knockouts,
   normal nutrients) gives identical EPS to baseline. The regulatory switch being
   "on" does not help without a carbon surplus to redirect. The combined condition
   is required for maximum EPS.

4. PNAG is suppressed by N-limitation (emergent result): PNAG dropped from
   0.676 at baseline to 0.200 under N-limitation and combined_EPS. This was not
   programmed explicitly -- it emerged because UDP-GlcNAc (PNAG monomer) contains
   nitrogen (N-acetyl group), so the model correctly restricts it when NH4+ is
   scarce. This is biologically consistent with literature.

5. Growth-EPS trade-off is real and unavoidable: all three pathways compete with
   biomass for the same precursor pool (UDP-glucose for cellulose and colanic acid,
   UDP-GlcNAc for PNAG). The production envelope shows this clearly.

MODELING NOTES AND CAVEATS
---------------------------
- All FBA results are UPPER BOUND predictions under the given nutrient constraints.
  Real cells will produce less due to regulatory overhead, maintenance costs, and
  post-transcriptional effects not captured in FBA.

- The c-di-GMP cofactor stoichiometry in CELSYNTH (0.001 per monomer) is a
  modeling approximation. In reality, c-di-GMP activates bcsA allosterically
  and is not consumed. The small stoichiometric coupling is used to force DGUNC
  (ydaM) to be active when cellulose is being made.

- COLASYNTH is a lumped reaction collapsing ~10 Wca enzymatic steps into one.
  This is standard for GEM pathway additions where intermediates are unknown.
  The stoichiometry is based on the known monomer composition of colanic acid
  (Stevenson et al. 1996) not on kinetic parameters.

- The PNAG pathway uses PUACGAMS in reverse for synthesis. The equilibrium of
  this reaction in vivo favors the direction written in the model (degradation).
  In the GEM, running in reverse is feasible as long as the thermodynamic
  constraint is not enforced -- standard FBA does not enforce thermodynamics.

- parFBA (parsimonious FBA) is used instead of standard FBA to avoid biologically
  unrealistic high-flux solutions. It minimizes total flux while achieving the
  optimal objective value.

- The iML1515 model file must be present at /Users/mayaabdalla/Downloads/iML1515.xml
  to run any notebook cells. It is not stored in the repository.
